# Practical guides and templates

The 7 practical guides linked in the source doc, plus the UXinsight briefing template. Each entry summarizes the substance the agent should be able to apply.

## 1. Conversation guide for onboarding to a new product team
- Best-match URL: https://medium.com/@MagdaJanowitz/mastering-onboarding-a-ux-research-forward-guide-to-human-friendly-integration-and-support-9359eecb0397 (Magda Janowicz)
- Companion: Leticia Ratkiewicz's "My first month as the new UX Researcher in a 40-people design team" (Nubank)
- Define project focus before promising deliverables — generative exploration vs. evaluative findings vs. stakeholder asks vs. marketing input vs. conceptual ideation.
- Lock objectives collaboratively with key stakeholders at the start.
- For new researchers: ask questions in design critiques, workshops, and research huddles — treat question-asking as a learning discipline.
- Claim small autonomous research tasks early to build a proactive reputation.
- Keep regular check-ins with stakeholders to track shifting objectives.
- Onboard on **people** first (names, roles, relationships) before subjects/projects; keep notes on people specifically.

## 2. How to create a practical user research plan + template
- URL: https://condens.io/blog/ux-user-research-plan-template/
- Five-section template:
  1. **Background / problem statement** — why the research is happening, the core question, stakeholder expectations.
  2. **Research goals and objectives** — specific, scoped, actionable, in stakeholders' language.
  3. **Methodology** — short description of methods, why each, duration, tools; cover primary and secondary research.
  4. **Timeline and budget** — when results land, plus budget approval if relevant.
  5. **Team and stakeholders** — title, date, team, stakeholders.
- Use the template as a shared kickoff doc, not an internal artifact only.

## 3. User research note-taking guide
- URLs: https://condens.io/user-research-note-taking/ + https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/research-notetaking-templates-methods
- Good notes need three elements: **context** (background to stand alone), **structure** (a template the moderator can scan), **evidence** (verbatim quotes, behaviour, not editorialized summaries).
- Replace conclusory notes ("user didn't like it") with observed-cause notes (which step, which control, what the user said).
- Standing template columns: timestamp, task/topic, observation, quote, severity/tag, follow-up question.
- Templates exist in Figma/FigJam and Miro — separate what users say vs. do.
- Solo-moderating without a template is unsustainable in real time.
- Pre-load the template with red flags and common patterns for fast tagging.

## 4. How to analyse user interviews
- URLs: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thematic-analysis/ + https://medium.com/the-interaction-design-foundation/how-to-do-a-thematic-analysis-of-user-interviews-9eb4a1ced06e
- Use **thematic analysis**: tag observations and quotes with codes, then cluster codes into themes.
- Six phases: (1) gather data, (2) read everything end-to-end, (3) code segments by topic, (4) generate candidate themes from related codes, (5) break and return with fresh eyes, (6) evaluate themes for fit, distinctness, and grounding.
- A **code** = short word/phrase label for a segment; shorthand-describes what it's about.
- Pair with affinity mapping: cut data into snippets and group by similarity.
- Cluster by content similarity, not by research question or sticky-note color.
- Label clusters with insight statements, not single-noun categories.
- Thematic analysis pairs naturally with grounded theory and scales to research-repository inputs.

## 5. How to do large-scale group analysis user research
- URL: https://userresearch.blog.gov.uk/2017/12/20/how-we-did-a-large-scale-group-analysis-of-user-research-data/ (UK Government Digital Service)
- GDS ran four 3-hour sessions with twelve cross-functional colleagues per session (PMs, service designers, stakeholders).
- Split each session into groups of three; give each person a highlighter and one research question.
- Each person individually highlights transcript sections relevant to their question; group members swap notes until every question is covered.
- Transfer highlights to Post-its color-coded by research question.
- During clustering, **drop the question colors** — let themes emerge from data, not from prompts.
- Label clusters with full insight statements rather than nouns.
- Run affinity diagrams to combine findings across groups.
- Pre-group raw data (by interview question, task, or screen) before the session so participants don't hunt.

## 6. Accessibility in Research
- Canonical URLs:
  - https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/guide-to-accessible-inclusive-ux-research-96aeb3a80d3d (Oxana Loseva)
  - https://medium.com/booking-research/building-accessibility-research-practices-75d82098f286 (Maya Alvarado, Booking.com)
  - https://medium.com/cvs-health-tech-blog/the-inclusive-ux-research-playbook-from-cvs-health-inclusive-design-fb652552929a (CVS Health)
- Accessibility research = regular UX research that intentionally includes disabled people — permanent, temporary, and situational impairments all count.
- Improvements surfaced by accessibility research typically benefit all users (clearer navigation helps everyone).
- Recruit by functional abilities and AT use, not diagnoses; screen for AT use, devices, comfort.
- Don't violate HIPAA or collect unnecessary PII.
- Pre-study contact: ask preferred communication channel, format, compensation, and accommodations.
- Physical venues: accessible elevators (large enough for participant + companion), accessible restrooms.
- Existing UX research skills transfer directly; no months-long ramp-up before starting.
- Always offer choice of remote vs. in-person; never force a modality.

## 7. Start-to-finish guide for conducting a diary study
- URL: https://dscout.com/people-nerds/diary-study-guide
- Five phases: planning, recruiting & onboarding, monitoring, debriefing, analysis.
- **Planning:** write research questions; decide open diary (generative, free-form) vs. closed diary (evaluative, structured prompts).
- **Data formats:** text, photo, video, audio — pick what matches the behavior being captured.
- **Recruiting:** screen by demographic, behavior, and study-specific criteria.
- **Onboarding:** clear instructions on platform, content expected, entry frequency; consider training session or written walkthrough.
- **Monitor entries** during the study — nudge silent participants, ask probing follow-ups on rich entries.
- **Debrief interviews** after the diary period contextualize and pressure-test entries.
- **Analysis:** dump entries into a spreadsheet for trends; map findings onto a chronological user journey to expose pain points and opportunities over time.
- Diary studies are longitudinal by design — typically days to weeks — and only work when participants can easily record the data being asked for.

## 8. UXinsight UX research briefing template
- URL: https://uxinsight.org/ux-research-briefing-template/  (PDF: https://uxinsight.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/UX-Research-Briefing-Template-UXinsight-by-Maaike-Mintjes.pdf)
- Author: Maaike Mintjes.
- Filled out collaboratively with team/client/stakeholders before kicking off research.
- Locks down budget, deadlines, and recruitment handling at the start.
- Aligns on **Why** (problem space), **What** (research questions and hypotheses), **How** (method, timeline, budget).
- Captures known assumptions and existing knowledge to avoid duplicate research.
- Identifies primary stakeholders, decision-makers, and audiences the findings need to reach.
- Functions as the contract / source-of-truth document referenced through the project.

## 9. UXinsight blog — "5 tips to land a first UX research job"
- URL: https://uxinsight.org/how-to-get-into-ux-research-5-tips-for-landing-your-first-job/
- **Tip 1 — Understand required skills:** read multiple UXR job descriptions, talk to working researchers, consult skills frameworks to map hiring teams' expectations.
- **Tip 2 — Translate existing experience:** identify transferable skills; rewrite resume bullets in UXR language matched to JD keywords.
- **Tip 3 — Inject UXR practice into your current job:** run small usability tests or interviews with your actual users where allowed.
- **Tip 4 — Take applied training:** pick courses with hands-on components, not just theory; or run a self-directed UX research project.
- **Tip 5 — Build a small portfolio:** ~5 PowerPoint slides per project — problem, methodology, outcome — short and digestible.
- Hiring managers in portfolio review want a story arc, not exhaustive documentation.
