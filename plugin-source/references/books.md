# Books

Every book from the source guide, plus comment-suggested additions. Each entry: one-line takeaway, author bio in one line, what it teaches, target reader, key concepts, signature contribution.

## Foundational

### Don't Make Me Think — Steve Krug (2000; Revisited 2014)
- URL: https://sensible.com/dont-make-me-think/
- One-liner: The foundational book on web usability arguing that interfaces should never force users to puzzle out how they work.
- Author: longtime usability consultant; worked with Apple, Bloomberg, NPR.
- Teaches: common-sense principles for self-evident pages, scanning, navigation, conventions, and how to run cheap DIY usability tests.
- Reader: designers, developers, PMs, anyone shipping an interface.
- Key concepts: satisficing, scannability and visual hierarchy, trunk test for navigation, three usability laws, low-cost qualitative testing.
- Contribution: popularised usability testing as cheap, frequent, informal — not a heavyweight lab exercise. 700k+ copies across editions.

### Just Enough Research — Erika Hall (A Book Apart, 2013; 2nd ed. 2019; Mule Books 2024)
- URL: https://abookapart.com/products/just-enough-research
- One-liner: Compact field manual that treats research as a habit any team member can build.
- Author: co-founded Mule Design Studio.
- Teaches: scoping research, running lightweight studies, surfacing bias, using findings to make decisions quickly.
- Reader: designers, founders, product teams without a dedicated researcher.
- Key concepts: organisational and competitive research, user and usability research, surfacing assumptions, why focus groups fail, JTBD (newer editions).
- Contribution: reframed research from academic exercise into everyday practice — origin of the "just enough" mindset.

### Think Like a UX Researcher — David Travis & Philip Hodgson (Routledge, 1st ed. 2019; 2nd ed. 2024)
- URL: https://www.routledge.com/Think-Like-a-UX-Researcher-How-to-Observe-Users-Influence-Design-and-Shape-Business-Strategy/Travis-Hodgson/p/book/9781032478487
- One-liner: Trains researchers to operate strategically, not just deliver studies on demand.
- Authors: Travis has run usability work since 1989 (PhD experimental psych); Hodgson has 25 years across medical devices, banking, consumer goods.
- Teaches: planning and running research, analysis, persuasion of stakeholders, growing a UX career — with exercises throughout.
- Reader: practicing UXRs and designers ready to move from tactical to strategic.
- Key concepts: strategy framing, observation skills, persuasion, UX maturity workshops, war stories; 2nd ed. adds essays on remote research limits, survey quality, epistemological bias.
- Contribution: frames research as a way of thinking and influencing the business.

### Interviewing Users: How to Uncover Compelling Insights — Steve Portigal (Rosenfeld Media, 1st ed. 2013; 2nd ed. 2023)
- URL: https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/interviewing-users-second-edition/
- One-liner: The definitive craft book on running user interviews that go beyond chit-chat to actual insight.
- Author: 25+ years interviewing audiences from credit-default-swap traders to rock musicians.
- Teaches: planning interview studies, question framing, rapport, note-taking, synthesis into stories.
- Reader: new and experienced researchers; designers and PMs who interview regularly.
- Key concepts: interviewing as a learned skill, bias, remote interviewing, ResearchOps, planning and logistics, analysis and synthesis, research impact.
- Contribution: established interviewing as a teachable discipline; 2nd ed. extends into team and ops practice.

### The Mom Test — Rob Fitzpatrick (Founder Centric, 2013; Simon & Schuster reissue 2024)
- URL: https://www.momtestbook.com/
- One-liner: A short, blunt guide to customer conversations that surface truth instead of polite encouragement.
- Author: founder, educator; years coaching startups on customer discovery.
- Teaches: how to talk about a customer's life and problems without asking leading questions about your idea.
- Reader: founders, PMs, indie hackers, anyone doing customer discovery without a research background.
- Key concepts: three rules (talk about life, ask about past, talk less); false positives like compliments and hypotheticals; commitment and currency; advancing toward sales.
- Contribution: gave the startup community a memorable framework for separating signal from noise.

## Methodology

### Universal Methods of Design, Expanded & Revised — Bella Martin & Bruce Hanington (Rockport 2012; Expanded 2019)
- URL: https://www.quarto.com/books/9781631597480/universal-methods-of-design-expanded-and-revised
- One-liner: A reference encyclopedia of 125 research, ideation, and synthesis methods in tight two-page spreads.
- Authors: Hanington heads School of Design at Carnegie Mellon; Martin is lead UXR with Microsoft Research, USPS, GSK experience.
- Teaches: recognise, choose, apply human-centred methods across the project lifecycle.
- Reader: designers, researchers, students, educators wanting a method picker.
- Key concepts: 125 methods covering ethnography, generative research, evaluation, participatory design, behavioural and attitudinal techniques; 25 new chapters in expanded ed. on digital/software topics.
- Contribution: set the standard format for method reference books.

### Doorbells, Danger, and Dead Batteries — Steve Portigal (Rosenfeld Media 2016)
- URL: https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/user-research-war-stories/
- One-liner: Field war stories that teach research craft through hard-won lessons from many practitioners.
- Teaches: handling the chaos of real fieldwork — awkward participants, broken logistics, ethical wobbles, safety, emotional fallout.
- Reader: researchers preparing for or recovering from messy field studies; managers building team resilience.
- Key concepts: 66 stories grouped around physical/emotional risk, participant behavior, stakeholder dynamics, logistics breakdowns, empathy.
- Contribution: legitimised storytelling and reflection as part of the researcher's craft.

### Surveys That Work — Caroline Jarrett (Rosenfeld Media 2021)
- URL: https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/surveys-that-work/
- One-liner: Practical playbook for designing surveys that produce decision-grade data.
- Author: forms/survey specialist with Oxford math degree.
- Teaches: 7-step process — goals, sampling, question wording, questionnaire design, fieldwork, response analysis, reporting.
- Reader: researchers, designers, analysts running quant studies without survey methodology training.
- Key concepts: Total Survey Error framework, "Survey Octopus," writing fair questions, sampling, "Least You Can Do" minimum-viable guidance.
- Contribution: translated rigorous survey methodology into accessible UX-flavoured practice.

### User Research: A Practical Guide — Stephanie Marsh (Kogan Page, 1st ed. 2018; 2nd 2022; 3rd 2025)
- URL: https://www.koganpage.com/marketing-communications/user-research-9781398625075
- One-liner: A method-by-method tour of UXR with deep attention to ethics, ops, and now AI.
- Author: led UXR at UK Government Digital Service; now Research Ops Lead at Springer Nature.
- Teaches: choosing/executing the right method, recruiting ethically, analysing data, sharing findings.
- Reader: new researchers, students, product folk needing a single reference; 3rd ed. adds value for mid-career practitioners.
- Key concepts: usability testing, card sorting, surveys, A/B testing, diary studies, ethics, recruitment, ResearchOps, responsible AI in each method.
- Contribution: one of the few practical guides treating ethics, accessibility, and ResearchOps as primary topics.

### It's Our Research — Tomer Sharon (Morgan Kaufmann 2012)
- URL: https://shop.elsevier.com/books/its-our-research/sharon/978-0-12-385130-7
- One-liner: First book dedicated to winning stakeholder buy-in for UXR instead of just doing the studies.
- Author: led UXR at Google, then WeWork and Goldman Sachs.
- Teaches: identifying research opportunities with stakeholders, planning studies together, communicating findings so they drive action.
- Reader: in-house UXRs working inside product orgs who feel sidelined.
- Key concepts: roles of business/engineering/UX stakeholders; partnership over delivery; nine signs research is making impact; communication of results; office politics.
- Contribution: reframed research success around organisational influence rather than method purity.

### User and Task Analysis for Interface Design — JoAnn Hackos & Janice (Ginny) Redish (Wiley 1998)
- URL: https://www.wiley.com/en-us/User+and+Task+Analysis+for+Interface+Design-p-9780471178316
- One-liner: Foundational text on structured site visits and task analysis before any design work begins.
- Authors: pioneers of usable design and technical communication research from the 1980s.
- Teaches: full lifecycle of user and task analysis — preparing site visits, observing, asking, recording, analysing, translating into prototypes, validating through usability tests.
- Reader: interface designers, technical communicators, researchers on enterprise or specialist software.
- Key concepts: user-centred design rationale, contextual observation, task decomposition, team selection and training, paper prototyping, integration with usability testing.
- Contribution: codified user and task analysis as a discipline; bridged human factors into software UX.

### Research Practice — Gregg Bernstein (self-published 2021)
- URL: https://gregg.io/book/
- One-liner: A behind-the-scenes look at what UXR's day-to-day actually looks like, told through practitioner stories.
- Author: research leader in media/publishing; mentors at First Round's Fast Track.
- Teaches: not methods — how to enter the field, start in a new role, build relationships, grow a practice.
- Reader: aspiring and early-career researchers; managers building teams.
- Key concepts: pathways into research, starting in a new role, building momentum, navigating tradeoffs, growing teams.
- Contribution: filled the gap between method books and career guides.

### The Moderator's Survival Guide — Donna Tedesco & Fiona Tranquada (Morgan Kaufmann 2013)
- URL: http://www.modsurvivalguide.org/
- One-liner: A scenario-by-scenario manual for handling awkward, tricky moments during user sessions.
- Teaches: recognising and responding to ~100 specific situations in usability tests, contextual inquiries, and interviews.
- Reader: new and experienced moderators wanting playbook responses.
- Key concepts: session control techniques, participant management, technical and logistical recovery, ethical decisions, 18 first-person survival stories.
- Contribution: first book to treat moderation itself as a teachable skill with named patterns.

## Quantitative

### Quantifying the User Experience — Jeff Sauro & James Lewis (Morgan Kaufmann, 1st 2012; 2nd 2016)
- URL: https://shop.elsevier.com/books/quantifying-the-user-experience/sauro/978-0-12-802308-2
- One-liner: Practical stats handbook for UX professionals who need to defend numbers, not just show them.
- Authors: Sauro founded MeasuringU (six-sigma trained); Lewis is senior IBM human factors engineer.
- Teaches: confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, sample-size formulas, standard questionnaires applied to typical UX questions.
- Reader: UX researchers, analysts, designers needing to run or read quant studies confidently.
- Key concepts: confidence intervals, comparing designs, sample size planning, SUS, correlation/regression/ANOVA.
- Contribution: made rigorous statistical practice accessible specifically to the UX community.

### Measuring the User Experience — Tom Tullis & Bill Albert (Morgan Kaufmann, 1st 2008; 2nd 2013; 3rd 2022)
- URL: https://shop.elsevier.com/books/measuring-the-user-experience/albert/978-0-12-818080-8
- One-liner: Reference for choosing, collecting, and presenting UX metrics across performance, attitude, and behaviour.
- Authors: Tullis long-time Fidelity UXR leader; Albert directs Bentley UX Center.
- Teaches: identifying, capturing, analysing, visualising metrics — task success, time on task, satisfaction, engagement, emotion.
- Reader: researchers, designers, analysts wanting a comprehensive metrics playbook with worked examples.
- Key concepts: performance, issues-based, self-reported, behavioural/physiological, and derived metrics; eye-tracking and emotion measurement; Excel analysis examples.
- Contribution: first and still-leading book to systematically catalog UX metrics; 3rd ed. won 2024 TAA Textbook Excellence Award.

### Benchmarking the User Experience — Jeff Sauro (MeasuringU Press 2018; updated 2020)
- URL: https://measuringu.com/book/benchmarking-the-user-experience/
- One-liner: Practical guide to setting up repeatable UX benchmark studies that track quality over time and against competitors.
- Author: Sauro has done benchmarking for Google, Facebook, eBay, PayPal.
- Teaches: planning benchmark studies, choosing metrics, recruiting, running waves, interpreting comparisons.
- Reader: UX researchers, product owners, designers needing to demonstrate progress or competitive position with numbers.
- Key concepts: benchmark study planning checklist, competitive benchmarking, common mistakes, sample forms and scripts.
- Contribution: first dedicated treatment of benchmarking as a distinct UXR practice.

## Career & related

### The User's Journey — Donna Lichaw (Rosenfeld Media 2016)
- URL: https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/storymapping/
- One-liner: Applies screenwriting story structure to product design so experiences have arcs, not flows.
- Author: former filmmaker turned product strategist and executive coach.
- Teaches: mapping onboarding, core experiences, and engagement loops as narrative arcs using Post-its-and-markers exercises.
- Reader: product designers, PMs, founders looking to align teams around a coherent user narrative.
- Key concepts: concept stories, origin stories, usage stories, narrative arc, climactic moments, engagement highs and lows.
- Contribution: brought classic story theory into product design vocabulary.

### I Want a UX Job! — Lauryl Zenobi (self-published 2020)
- **Correction:** the author's name is spelled **Lauryl** (not "Laurel").
- URL: https://www.iwantauxjob.com/
- One-liner: A career-change handbook for breaking into UX research from academia or any other field.
- Author: Principal UXR with an anthropology background; works on human-centred design for federal agencies.
- Teaches: building research skills, joining the community, assembling a portfolio, writing a CV/resume, interviewing for UXR roles.
- Reader: PhDs leaving academia, career changers from social sciences.
- Key concepts: skills inventory and gap analysis, community participation, portfolio building, CV-to-resume translation, interview prep, worksheets.
- Contribution: first book focused specifically on becoming a UX researcher rather than doing the work.

### Ask For It — Linda Babcock & Sara Laschever (Bantam 2008)
- URL: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/6885/ask-for-it-by-linda-babcock-and-sara-laschever/
- One-liner: Step-by-step program for negotiating for yourself; follow-up to *Women Don't Ask*.
- Authors: Babcock is negotiation economist (CMU prof); Laschever is journalist who led interviews for Harvard's Project Access.
- Teaches: a four-phase program — recognising what you deserve, building bargaining power, choosing a strategy, managing emotional dynamics.
- Reader: women negotiating salary, promotion, scope, or domestic labour; allies and managers.
- Key concepts: identifying negotiable situations, market and personal data, BATNA-style preparation, gendered reactions, scripts and stories from real women.
- Contribution: translated research on the gender ask gap into actionable personal-development program.

## Free / open

### Qualitative Research Practice Guide — Code for America (free PDF, 2019)
- URL: https://info.codeforamerica.org/qualitative-research
- PDF: https://f.hubspotusercontent30.net/hubfs/5622333/CFA_QualitativeResearchGuide_v1.pdf
- One-liner: Free, government-services-focused guide to ethical, trauma-aware qualitative research with vulnerable users.
- Authored by Code for America Qualitative Research team; 2019.
- Teaches: philosophy and methods for researching with people navigating public benefits, criminal justice, and other high-stakes services.
- Reader: civic technologists, government UX teams, nonprofit researchers, anyone studying vulnerable users.
- Key concepts: core research philosophy, ethics and consent, trauma-informed and healing-centred research, planning and executing field studies, synthesis into action, remote-research adaptations.
- Contribution: one of the most thorough public-sector research playbooks; foregrounds ethics and power dynamics.

### "List of 100 Behavioural Design Books" — Samuel Salzer (Habit Weekly)
- URL: https://www.habitweekly.com/behavioral-books
- Notion (updated): https://habitweekly.notion.site/b26a66109c73406d83059276cfaceaa2
- One-liner: A curated 100-book reading guide assembled by behavioural designer Samuel Salzer.
- Coverage: behavioural economics, cognitive and social psychology, habit formation, choice architecture, applied design.
- Reader: UXRs, product designers, behavioural designers wanting a curriculum.
- Key sources cited: built with input from Richard Thaler, Dan Ariely, Susan Weinschenk, Robert Cialdini, Tali Sharot.
- Contribution: de facto canonical reading list for the behavioural design community.
- Adjacent: Aim For Behavior's 150+ books list extends Salzer's into service design and systems thinking.
