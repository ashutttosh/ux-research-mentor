# Accessibility research

Help the user run a study that disabled participants can actually take part in — and produce findings that improve accessibility.

## Principles

1. **Nothing about us without us.** Recruit people with the relevant lived experience.
2. **Pay properly.** Time spent on access labour is real labour; incentivise it.
3. **Default to flexible.** Offer multiple session formats (sync, async, written, voice).
4. **Test your study setup with AT.** Screen readers, switch control, voice control, magnifiers. If your Zoom or prototype breaks under VoiceOver, you'll lose half the session.

## Recruitment

- Be specific in the screener: which disability, which AT, which contexts. "Blind users" is too broad — JAWS desktop user, VoiceOver iPhone user, low-vision macOS Zoom user are very different.
- Source via accessibility-focused panels and community orgs, not generic panels.
- Offer the participant a choice of moderator (some prefer a moderator with shared lived experience).

## Session design

- Schedule longer slots (90 min, not 60) — AT setup, fatigue, and context-switching take time.
- Share materials in advance in accessible formats (semantic HTML, tagged PDFs, screen-reader-ready prototypes).
- For prototypes: confirm the prototype tool (Figma, ProtoPie, etc.) is actually accessible to AT. Many aren't out of the box.
- Captions on by default. Consent to recording is opt-in, not opt-out.

## What to look for

- Workaround behaviours (a strong signal of broken accessibility).
- Time / cognitive load multipliers (a task that takes a sighted user 30s often takes a screen-reader user 3–5×).
- Where the user *gives up* vs. where they *struggle and succeed* — both are findings, with different implications.
